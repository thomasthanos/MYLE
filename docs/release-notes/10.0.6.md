# MYLE 10.0.6

- **Fix unlocking MYLE on Android.** MYLE no longer offers its own autofill prompt for its master password or other vault fields. This prevents the misleading website/app authorization error when selecting “Unlock MYLE” inside MYLE itself. No autofill service, MYLE's or another's, can fill or save what you type in MYLE's own screens.
- **Passkeys on Android 14+.** MYLE now identifies the browser or app that asks from the signing certificate Android supplies. Android hides most other apps from MYLE, so creating and using passkeys and passwords through Android's credential providers could fail before. A page that names no relying party gets the WebAuthn default: its own website.
- **Saving passwords from other apps.** Android's “Save to MYLE” could not check which app asked on Android 11 and later; it can now.
- **Settings on the phone.** The Account sheet is now Settings, in four groups: Security & vault (lock time, opening with your fingerprint or face, changing the master password), Autofill & passkeys (each shown On or Off, opening Android's own settings), Sync & backup, and About (version and update check). Every control is at least 48 px. On a phone these settings left the ⋯ menu, which links to Settings instead.
- **Phone layouts.** Search and all three toolbar actions fit narrow screens. Password-generator and More menus fit the available width. The welcome page scrolls on short screens; its privacy information remains reachable.
- **Native verification screens.** Improved spacing and button readability. Failed autofill identity checks offer a retry that repeats the same website/app validation, and `adb logcat -s MYLE` shows why a request stopped. Removed expensive full-screen backdrop blur and repeated panel entrance animations on mobile.

The Android Credential Manager provider requires Android 14 or later. On Android 11, passwords and saved TOTP codes use Android Autofill. Fingerprint availability depends on the device's enrolled strong biometric. Physical Samsung A22 behavior still needs verification on the phone; browser layout checks and native tests do not replace device testing.
